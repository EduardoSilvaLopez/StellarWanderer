This is the second biggest political entity of the setting, with 15 stellar systems with cities in the year 700.

It was created by the Founders, a group of believers, about 140 years ago.

Its most important political principles are:
* Human Rights, which includes the provision of 'all needs' (meaning a minimum standard of living). They use the slogan
'From each by their skills, to each by their needs' and also political participation in assemblies in basic democratic
institutions, including all means of productions (for example, the workers of a factory or the staff of an outpost).
* Collective property of all means of production, up to the smallest industry or workshop. This combines the strong
influence and power of the workers there, with a de-jure property by the State alone, which can intervene in
extraordinary cases.
* Empathy as the most important human trait. Solidarity, generosity, sacrifice for the community... are highly praised.
Individualism is frowned upon.
* Social reputation as a means to distribute wealth beyond the needs.
* Social pressure as a means to enforce the law, instead of force, which is used only as last resort.
* More skills means more obligations, but also more reputation.
* Anti-capitalism: Money is banned. Stamps are used as equivalent, but they have no denomination ("stamp with a value
of 5 stamps" do not exist) and are physical. Thus, they are only useful for very small transactions corresponding to the
very limited property allowed: clothes, food, personal devices.
* Pride in not producing 'useless things', by which they often mean cultural and artistic products of any kind, unless
they serve the political propaganda.

People of very low reputation can be sent to "learn again the values of society" becoming "Students" and thus of the same rank as real students. People who keep
misbehaving are degraded to "Dependent", which is the rank of children, and in this case means they are under
surveillance because they "need special care". Serious crimes make them "prisoners", the lowest rank, and they
are then actually imprisoned and forced to work for the State.

Highly reputed people, on the other side, get better housing, the de-facto permanent use of luxury items and special
services. Reputation is indicated by the title used to address them, which is not strictly uniform (the personal
opinion of each one can change the title they use towards others) but has a large consensus.

Those-Who-Share has the biggest heavy industry of mankind, and also excel in the exploitation of natural resources.
They lack severely of cultural production and soft power outside their territory, where propaganda does work to an
extent: People in Those-Who-Share are usually proud of their sense of a common vision and purpose for the whole
"Community", a very important term meaning all citizens.

Points which are critisised overseas and also by their own rebels:
* The discouragement of individual wishes and tastes.
* The discouragement of artistic production, the love of "dull" decoration, landscapes and architecture.
* The social pressure people are submitted to.
* The forced labour for prisoners and the "re-education camps" for adult "Students".
* The percieved hypocrisy of the luxuries enjoyed by the highly reputed citizens, which contrasts with a percieved
egalitarianism (note however that egalitarianism as such is not explicitly a principle of Those-Who-Share).
* Their perceived isolationism.
